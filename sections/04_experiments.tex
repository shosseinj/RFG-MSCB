\section{Experiments}\label{experiments}

\subsection{Datasets and Data Splits}\label{dataset}

To evaluate the proposed framework under a fixed training and evaluation design, we
used Kvasir-SEG \cite{kvasirseg}, CVC-ClinicDB \cite{cvcclinicdb},
CVC-300 \cite{cvc300}, CVC-ColonDB \cite{cvccolondb}, and
ETIS-LaribPolypDB \cite{etis}. Kvasir-SEG and CVC-ClinicDB were split
independently with a 90:10 ratio using shuffled sampling with random seed
42. Their training subsets were merged, giving 900 Kvasir-SEG and 550
CVC-ClinicDB images for training. The corresponding held-out validation subsets of
100 and 62 images were merged for checkpoint selection and were also evaluated
separately for reporting. CVC-300, CVC-ColonDB, and ETIS-LaribPolypDB
were used only for external evaluation; none influenced training or
checkpoint selection.

Images were converted to RGB, resized to $352\times352$ with bilinear

ImageNet normalization with mean $(0.485,0.456,0.406)$ and standard
deviation $(0.229,0.224,0.225)$.

\subsection{Implementation Details}\label{implementation-details}

The three final runs used seeds 42, 6543, and 7777. All final and ablation
results are reported as the mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation (ddof=1) across
these seeds unless otherwise noted. The runs were implemented
in PyTorch 2.5.1+cu124 on Windows, with a maximum of 200 epochs and batch size 24.
AdamW with betas
$(0.9,0.999)$ used separate parameter groups. The maximum learning rates
were $3.0\times10^{-4}$ for the decoder and $4.5\times10^{-4}$ for the
final refinement head. Encoder Stages 4, 3, 2, and 1 plus the stem used
$3.0\times10^{-5}$, $2.4\times10^{-5}$, $1.92\times10^{-5}$, and
$1.536\times10^{-5}$, respectively, corresponding to layer-wise decay
0.8. Weight decay was $1.0\times10^{-4}$ for the decoder,
$5.0\times10^{-2}$ for the encoder, and $1.0\times10^{-2}$ for the
refinement head.

Learning rates were warmed up linearly for five epochs from 0.1 to 1.0
of each group maximum and then cosine-annealed over the remaining epochs
to $1.0\times10^{-6}$. Training used AMP FP16, global gradient clipping
at norm 1.0, and an exponential moving average (EMA) with decay 0.995.
All parameters were trainable from the first epoch; no staged freezing
or decoder-only warmup was used. The encoder was initialized from
\texttt{convnext\_tiny\_22k\_1k\_384.pth}, logged as ImageNet pretrained
weights. Deep supervision and frequency augmentation were disabled, and
sampling was uniform. Early stopping was configured with patience 30
from the start of training. Seeds 42 and 6543 completed the maximum 200
epochs, corresponding to zero-based epochs 0--199; seed 7777 stopped after
epoch 167 (168 completed epochs). Model complexity and
its measurement convention are reported in Table~\ref{tab:model-complexity}.

Training augmentation applied horizontal and vertical flips independently
with probability 0.5, rotation in $[-15^{\circ},15^{\circ}]$ with
probability 0.5, and a random crop ratio in $[0.7,1.0]$ followed by
resizing with probability 0.5. Joint brightness and contrast factors in
$[0.85,1.15]$ and saturation in $[0.9,1.1]$ were applied with probability
0.5. A $3\times3$ Gaussian blur and additive Gaussian noise with standard
deviation 0.015 were applied with probabilities 0.15 and 0.25,
respectively.

The code seeded Python, NumPy, PyTorch CPU, and all CUDA random-number generators.
Deterministic CUDA algorithms and cuDNN deterministic backend flags were not enabled.

\subsection{Loss Function}\label{loss-function}

The final objective was
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=0.55\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Dice}}
+0.25\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BCE}}
+0.20\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{boundary}}.
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
The objective in Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss} uses a soft Dice term with a smoothing constant of 1.0. The BCE-with-logits
term used targets smoothed as
$y_{\mathrm{BCE}}=0.98y+0.01$, corresponding to label smoothing 0.02.
For the boundary-aware term, fixed Sobel filters were applied to each binary mask to
obtain $g=\sqrt{g_x^2+g_y^2}$. The magnitude was normalized per image as
$a=g/\max(\max(g),10^{-6})$, and the pixel weights were
$w=1+\kappa a$ with $\kappa=5$; consequently, the weights range from 1 to 6.
For probability $p=\sigma(P)$ and target $y$, the term was
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{boundary}}
=-\frac{\sum_i w_i[y_i\log(p_i+\epsilon)+(1-y_i)\log(1-p_i+\epsilon)]}
{\sum_i w_i+\epsilon}
+1-\frac{\sum_i w_i y_i p_i+\epsilon}
{\sum_i w_i(y_i+p_i-y_ip_i)+\epsilon},
\label{eq:boundary_loss}
\end{equation}
with $\epsilon=10^{-6}$ and batch averaging for the weighted IoU component. The
focal-Tversky term was assigned zero weight and therefore did not contribute to the
final objective.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}\label{evaluation-metrics}

We report mean Dice (mDice), mean intersection over union (mIoU),
S-measure $S_{\alpha}$ \cite{smeasure}, weighted F-measure
$F_{\beta}^{w}$ \cite{weightedfmeasure}, mean and maximum E-measure ($mE_{\phi}$ and
$\max E_{\phi}$) \cite{emeasure}, and mean absolute error (MAE)
\cite{saliencyfilters}. For each image, the thresholded prediction $P$
and binary ground truth $G$ were flattened and evaluated as
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Dice}(P,G)=
\frac{2|P\cap G|+10^{-6}}{|P|+|G|+10^{-6}},
\qquad
\operatorname{IoU}(P,G)=
\frac{|P\cap G|+10^{-6}}{|P|+|G|-|P\cap G|+10^{-6}}.
\label{eq:dice_iou}
\end{equation}
The reported mDice and mIoU are arithmetic means of the per-image scores in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dice_iou}, rather than global pixel aggregates. S-measure, weighted
F-measure, the E-measure curve, and MAE were computed per image with
\texttt{py\_sod\_metrics} from 8-bit probability maps and binary ground
truth. The mean and maximum of the resulting E-measure curve were
reported.

\subsection{Model Selection and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{model-selection}

At each epoch, EMA weights were evaluated on the merged held-out
Kvasir-SEG and CVC-ClinicDB validation set. Probabilities were obtained
with one sigmoid operation and binarized using the strict rule
$p>0.45$. A checkpoint was saved only when validation IoU was strictly
greater than the previous best. The selected checkpoints were epochs
184, 175, and 137 for seeds 42, 6543, and 7777, respectively. Final
evaluation loaded each best checkpoint strictly and evaluated the two
held-out subsets separately, followed by the full CVC-300,
CVC-ColonDB, and ETIS-LaribPolypDB sets. Test-time augmentation was
disabled, and external test results did not affect checkpoint selection.

\subsection{Comparative study}\label{comparative-study}

After the component-level analysis, the comparative study evaluates the complete model
on the held-out validation subsets and external datasets and places its performance
alongside published baselines. The quantitative results are presented below.

\subsubsection{Quantitative analysis}\label{quantitative-analysis}

On Kvasir-SEG, the proposed model achieved the highest displayed mDice and mIoU,
reaching $0.931\pm0.004$ and $0.883\pm0.006$, respectively. The closest displayed
method, PFPRNet, reported mDice of 0.930 and mIoU of 0.881. On CVC-ClinicDB,
the model remained competitive with recent methods, achieving $0.937\pm0.002$
mDice and $0.888\pm0.004$ mIoU; PFPRNet reported values 1.20 and 1.53 percentage
points higher, respectively.

The Kvasir-SEG and CVC-ClinicDB values come from held-out validation subsets involved
in checkpoint selection, whereas published methods may use different training and test
protocols. Across the comparison tables, the best and second-best displayed values
are shown in bold and underlined, respectively.

On the external datasets, mDice/mIoU were
$0.882\pm0.002$/$0.810\pm0.002$ on CVC-300,
$0.794\pm0.009$/$0.714\pm0.009$ on CVC-ColonDB, and
$0.787\pm0.011$/$0.707\pm0.012$ on ETIS-LaribPolypDB. The largest between-seed
variation in mDice and mIoU occurred on ETIS.
