\section{Discussion}\label{discussion}

\subsection{Main Quantitative Findings}

The final FAFEM + RFG-MSCB model achieves a Kvasir-SEG mDice of 0.9313 ± 0.0044 and mIoU of 0.8826 ± 0.0062 across three random seeds (42, 6543, 7777). On the held-out CVC-ClinicDB, it reaches 0.9370 ± 0.0024 mDice and 0.8877 ± 0.0038 mIoU. These results are reported as mean ± sample standard deviation (ddof=1). On external datasets, the model obtains 0.8821 ± 0.0016 mDice on CVC-300, 0.7944 ± 0.0089 on CVC-ColonDB, and 0.7870 ± 0.0113 on ETIS-LaribPolypDB. It does not attain the highest displayed mDice on the external datasets relative to the listed published comparators. No statistical significance testing was performed; all comparisons are descriptive.

\subsection{Effect of FAFEM}

The ablation chain begins with the FAFEM-only configuration. FAFEM is prior work, not a novel contribution of this paper; it is included to establish the final-protocol reference. Its Kvasir-SEG means are 0.925958 mDice, 0.875879 mIoU, and 0.024832 MAE. A no-FAFEM baseline under this final protocol is unavailable; older baseline measurements are protocol-mismatched and are not used here.

\subsection{Effect of Stage-3 MSCB-Lite}

Adding a Stage-3 MSCB-lite layer to the FAFEM-enhanced backbone gives the FAFEM + MSCB configuration. MSCB-lite is a prior multi-scale refinement module without frequency-guided coupling. On Kvasir-SEG, the three-seed mean mDice changes from 0.925958 for FAFEM to 0.930440 for FAFEM + MSCB (+0.448 percentage points). The mIoU changes by +0.550 percentage points and MAE by -0.000345. This configuration establishes the baseline against which the frequency-guided variant is evaluated.

\subsection{Effect of Residual Frequency-Guided Coupling}

The final FAFEM + RFG-MSCB model introduces residual frequency-guided coupling on top of the Stage-3 multi-scale layer. This is the principal architecture change proposed in this work. From raw three-seed values, Kvasir-SEG mDice changes from 0.930440 for FAFEM + MSCB to 0.931284 for RFG-MSCB (+0.084 percentage points); mIoU changes by +0.122 percentage points and MAE changes by -0.000527. The total FAFEM-to-final changes are +0.533 percentage points in mDice and +0.672 percentage points in mIoU. A single-seed direct frequency-guidance configuration did not exceed the Stage-3 MSCB-lite configuration in the paired seed-42 comparison, but this does not disentangle residualization from frequency conditioning.

\subsection{Why Residual Frequency-Guided Multi-Scale Refinement May Help}

The residual formulation bounds the guidance signal to a controlled interpolation between uniform and predicted branch weights (initial strength 0.05, init std 0.0). This design may mitigate instability observed in direct frequency guidance by providing a stable initialization and gradual adaptation during training. The frequency descriptor from FAFEM conditions the branch weighting in the MSCB-lite, potentially aligning multi-scale spatial features with complementary frequency-domain information. However, this interpretation is post-hoc; the ablation isolates the combined effect of the coupled mechanism but does not disentangle individual components (residual form, initialization strength, frequency conditioning).

\subsection{Cross-Dataset Behavior}

The final model's absolute performance is highest on Kvasir-SEG and held-out CVC-ClinicDB (~0.93), intermediate on CVC-300 (~0.88), and lower on CVC-ColonDB and ETIS-LaribPolypDB (~0.79). Between-seed variability is lowest on CVC-300 (mDice SD 0.0016) and highest on ETIS-LaribPolypDB (mDice SD 0.0113). Relative to FAFEM + MSCB, the final guidance change is not uniformly positive across datasets in the paired seed-42 evidence, so broad cross-dataset generalization should not be inferred.

\subsection{Cases and Datasets Where the Model Is Weaker}

The model's weakest performance is on CVC-ColonDB and ETIS-LaribPolypDB, where mDice drops to ~0.79 and between-seed variability is highest (0.0089 and 0.0113 respectively). These datasets involve different endoscopic systems and polyp morphology distributions compared to the training data (Kvasir-SEG and CVC-ClinicDB). On ETIS, the mIoU SD of 0.0120 indicates substantial sensitivity to random initialization. The model does not outperform several recent methods on these datasets, suggesting that further domain adaptation or data augmentation strategies may be needed for stronger generalization.

\subsection{Model Complexity and Practical Trade-offs}

As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:model-complexity}, the final architecture has a moderate parameter count within the small subset of methods for which primary-source counts could be verified. The ablation indicates a 2.4\% parameter increase from the FAFEM configuration to the final model. Computational-cost rankings remain uncertain because most competitor reports do not provide a verified input resolution together with an explicit FLOP-counting convention. No matched-hardware latency benchmark was performed, so the present evidence does not establish real-time inference suitability.

\subsection{Placement Analysis}

The multi-scale refinement layer is placed at encoder Stage 3 in the final FAFEM + RFG-MSCB configuration. This placement was selected based on observed results from the ablation chain. A single-seed exploratory all-skip configuration used batch size 32 rather than 24 in the main protocol; the available training evidence did not improve relative to the Stage-3 configuration. However, this comparison is limited by incomplete training and the batch-size mismatch, so it does not support a general conclusion about optimal placement. The Stage-3 placement localizes the added guidance mechanism; the measured final model has 30,057,415 parameters and 27.818 GFLOPs under the reported convention, but no latency benchmark was performed.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No statistical significance testing:} All reported differences are descriptive means over three seeds; no hypothesis testing was performed.
\item \textbf{Single-seed placement evidence:} The exploratory all-skip placement run is single-seed, incomplete, and uses a different batch size; it cannot establish a general placement rule.
\item \textbf{No disentanglement of RFG-MSCB components:} The ablation isolates the combined residual frequency-guided coupling; individual contributions of the residual formulation, initialization strength, and frequency conditioning are not separately measured.
\item \textbf{Incomplete experiments:} The residual-guidance run with default initialization did not complete training; its potential findings are unavailable.
\item \textbf{Domain gap on external datasets:} Performance drops substantially on CVC-ColonDB and ETIS-LaribPolypDB, indicating limited out-of-distribution generalization.
\item \textbf{Hardware and software not fully verified:} GPU model, CUDA/cuDNN versions, and \texttt{py\_sod\_metrics} version are not formally recorded.
\item \textbf{Training duration:} Seed 7777 stopped early at epoch 167 (patience 30), while seeds 42 and 6543 reached epoch 199; this asymmetry may affect the mean estimate.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Future Work}

\begin{itemize}
\item Systematic multi-seed placement ablation with controlled batch size and learning rate.
\item Disentanglement of the RFG-MSCB components (residual form, initialization schedule, frequency conditioning) via factorial experiments.
\item Investigation of initialization sensitivity using completed default-initialization runs.
\item Domain adaptation or targeted augmentation to improve generalization on CVC-ColonDB and ETIS-LaribPolypDB.
\item Statistical significance testing with larger seed ensembles or bootstrap confidence intervals.
\item Formal recording of hardware, software, and dependency versions for full reproducibility.
\end{itemize}
