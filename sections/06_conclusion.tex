\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

We presented a ConvNeXt-Tiny U-shaped segmentation framework that introduces a cross-level frequency-guided multi-scale refinement mechanism. The model integrates a bottleneck Frequency-Aware Feature Enhancement Module (FAFEM) with a Residual Frequency-Guided Multi-Scale Convolution Block (RFG-MSCB) placed at the Stage-3 encoder--decoder fusion. The frequency descriptor extracted by FAFEM is propagated to the decoder and used to condition the branch weights of the MSCB through a bounded residual formulation.

The RFG-MSCB preserves the conventional multi-scale pathway as a reference and learns a controlled, sample-dependent deviation guided by the frequency descriptor. This coupling connects deep semantic frequency information with higher-resolution spatial feature processing, while the remaining decoder stages are kept lightweight.

Ablation experiments using three random seeds show that the addition of the Stage-3 MSCB-lite to a FAFEM baseline increases Kvasir-SEG mDice by +0.34 percentage points. The further addition of the residual frequency-guided coupling yields a final Kvasir-SEG mDice of 0.9313 ± 0.0044, an additional +0.19 pp over the MSCB-lite configuration. On external datasets, the model achieves mDice of 0.9370 on CVC-ClinicDB, 0.8821 on CVC-300, 0.7944 on CVC-ColonDB, and 0.7870 on ETIS-LaribPolypDB. Performance on CVC-ColonDB and ETIS remains lower, indicating limited out-of-distribution generalization.

The final architecture adds 2.4\% in parameters relative to the FAFEM-only configuration; its detailed complexity and measurement convention are reported in Table~\ref{tab:model-complexity}. All performance values are descriptive means over three seeds; no statistical significance testing was performed.

In summary, this work demonstrates that cross-level frequency guidance with bounded residual branch adaptation is a viable design for polyp segmentation. Future work includes systematic placement ablation, component disentanglement of the RFG-MSCB, and domain adaptation strategies for stronger generalization on challenging external datasets.
