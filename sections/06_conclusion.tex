\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

We presented a ConvNeXt-Tiny U-shaped segmentation framework that introduces cross-level frequency-guided coupling between prior FAFEM and MSCB modules. The RFG-MSCB mechanism is placed at the Stage-3 encoder--decoder fusion, where the FAFEM frequency descriptor conditions MSCB branch weights through a bounded residual formulation.

The RFG-MSCB preserves the conventional multi-scale pathway as a reference and learns a controlled, sample-dependent deviation guided by the frequency descriptor. This coupling connects deep semantic frequency information with higher-resolution spatial feature processing, while the architectural modification remains localized to Stage 3.

Ablation experiments using three random seeds show that adding Stage-3 MSCB-lite changes Kvasir-SEG mDice from 0.925958 to 0.930440 (+0.448 percentage points). Adding residual frequency-guided coupling then changes the raw three-seed mean from 0.930440 to 0.931284 (+0.084 percentage points), with a +0.122 percentage-point mIoU change and a -0.000527 MAE change. On external datasets, the final model achieves mDice of 0.9370 on CVC-ClinicDB, 0.8821 on CVC-300, 0.7944 on CVC-ColonDB, and 0.7870 on ETIS-LaribPolypDB. Performance on CVC-ColonDB and ETIS remains lower, indicating limited out-of-distribution generalization.

The final architecture adds 2.4\% in parameters relative to the FAFEM-only configuration; its detailed complexity and measurement convention are reported in Table~\ref{tab:model-complexity}. All performance values are descriptive means over three seeds; no statistical significance testing was performed.

In summary, this work demonstrates a cross-level frequency-guided, bounded-residual refinement design with adaptive MSCB branch weighting, localized Stage-3 modification, and moderate measured parameter overhead. The descriptive ablation evidence supports this as a viable refinement direction, but does not establish uniform gains or statistical superiority. Future work includes systematic placement ablation, component disentanglement of RFG-MSCB, and domain adaptation for challenging external datasets.
