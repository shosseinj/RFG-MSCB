\section{Method}

\subsection{Overall Architecture}

The proposed framework follows a U-shaped encoder--decoder architecture built upon a ConvNeXt-Tiny encoder. The central objective is to exploit frequency information extracted from deep semantic features to adaptively regulate multi-scale feature processing during decoding. Rather than independently applying frequency enhancement and multi-scale convolution, the proposed model establishes a cross-level interaction between the bottleneck and the first decoder stage.

Let

$$
I \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times H\times W}
$$

denote an input colonoscopy image. The ConvNeXt-Tiny encoder extracts four hierarchical feature maps,

$$
\{F_1,F_2,F_3,F_4\}=E(I),
$$

where

$$
F_1\in\mathbb{R}^{96\times H/4\times W/4},\quad
F_2\in\mathbb{R}^{192\times H/8\times W/8},
$$

$$
F_3\in\mathbb{R}^{384\times H/16\times W/16},\quad
F_4\in\mathbb{R}^{768\times H/32\times W/32}.
$$

For an input resolution of $352\times352$, their spatial dimensions are $88\times88$, $44\times44$, $22\times22$, and $11\times11$, respectively.

The deepest encoder feature $F_4$ is first processed by a Frequency-Aware Feature Enhancement Module (FAFEM), which produces an enhanced bottleneck representation together with a compact frequency descriptor. The enhanced representation is passed through a lightweight residual bottleneck, whereas the frequency descriptor is propagated to the first decoder stage. After the decoder feature is fused with $F_3$, a Residual Frequency-Guided Multi-Scale Convolution Block (RFG-MSCB) uses the frequency descriptor to adaptively regulate its multi-scale convolution branches.

The overall processing can therefore be summarized as

$$
(\widetilde{F}_4,\mathbf{z}_f)
=
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FAFEM}}(F_4),
$$

$$
B=\mathcal{B}(\widetilde{F}_4),
$$

$$
D_4=
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{RFG}}
\left(
\mathcal{S}_3
\left[
\mathcal{D}_4(B),F_3
\right],
\mathbf{z}_f
\right),
$$

followed by

$$
D_3=
\mathcal{S}_2
\left[
\mathcal{D}_3(D_4),F_2
\right],
$$

$$
D_2=
\mathcal{S}_1
\left[
\mathcal{D}_2(D_3),F_1
\right],
$$

$$
D_1=\mathcal{D}_1(D_2).
$$

Here, $\mathcal{D}_i$ denotes a decoder block, $\mathcal{S}_i$ denotes the corresponding skip-feature fusion operation, $\mathbf{z}*f$ is the frequency descriptor extracted at the bottleneck, and $\mathcal{M}*{\mathrm{RFG}}$ denotes the proposed residual frequency-guided multi-scale refinement mechanism.

The final prediction head transforms $D_1$ into a single-channel segmentation logit map, which is bilinearly resized to the original image resolution.

\subsection{ConvNeXt-Tiny Encoder}

The encoder employs ConvNeXt-Tiny with stage depths

$$
[3,3,9,3]
$$

and channel dimensions

$$
[96,192,384,768].
$$

Before entering the encoder, each RGB image is normalized using the ImageNet statistics

$$
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(0.485,0.456,0.406),
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=(0.229,0.224,0.225).
$$

The first stage uses a $4\times4$ convolution with stride 4 to generate $F_1$. Each subsequent stage begins with spatial downsampling using a $2\times2$ stride-2 convolution.

For an input feature $X$, a ConvNeXt block first performs a $7\times7$ depthwise convolution. The resulting feature is normalized in channels-last format and processed by a pointwise multilayer perceptron with a fourfold channel expansion:

$$
Z=\mathrm{DWConv}_{7\times7}(X),
$$

$$
V=W_2
\left(
\mathrm{GELU}
\left(
W_1(\mathrm{LN}(Z))
\right)
\right).
$$

The block output is obtained through layer-scaled residual learning,

$$
Y=X+\mathrm{DropPath}(\gamma\odot V),
$$

where $\gamma$ is a learnable layer-scale parameter initialized to $10^{-6}$.

A stochastic-depth rate of 0.25 is used throughout the encoder. Feature dropout with probability 0.2 is additionally applied after the second, third, and fourth encoder stages.

\subsection{Frequency-Aware Bottleneck Enhancement}

The deepest encoder representation $F_4$ contains strong semantic information but has undergone substantial spatial downsampling. To expose complementary frequency characteristics of this representation, FAFEM is applied exclusively at the bottleneck.

Given

$$
X=F_4,
$$

a local low-frequency approximation is obtained using $3\times3$ average pooling,

$$
X_L=\mathrm{AvgPool}_{3\times3}(X),
$$

and the corresponding high-frequency component is defined as

$$
X_H=X-X_L.
$$

The low- and high-frequency components are independently transformed using depthwise convolutional branches,

$$
\widehat{X}_L=
\phi_L(X_L),
\qquad
\widehat{X}_H=
\phi_H(X_H),
$$

where each $\phi(\cdot)$ contains a $3\times3$ depthwise convolution followed by GELU activation.

The two components are concatenated and globally pooled to obtain the frequency descriptor

$$
\mathbf{z}_f
=
\mathrm{GAP}
\left(
[\widehat{X}_L,\widehat{X}_H]
\right)
\in\mathbb{R}^{2C}.
$$

Since $C=768$ at the bottleneck,

$$
\mathbf{z}_f\in\mathbb{R}^{1536}.
$$

A lightweight channel-gating network generates adaptive weights for the low- and high-frequency representations. Their weighted combination is spatially refined and introduced through a residual connection:

$$
\widetilde{F}_4
=
F_4+
\gamma_f\,\mathcal{R}_f
\left(
w_L\odot\widehat{X}_L+
w_H\odot\widehat{X}_H
\right),
$$

where $\gamma_f$ is a learnable scalar initialized to zero.

Importantly, FAFEM itself is not introduced in this work. In the proposed architecture, its frequency descriptor $\mathbf{z}_f$ is additionally reused to condition multi-scale processing at a later decoder stage.

\subsection{Lightweight Residual Bottleneck}

The frequency-enhanced representation $\widetilde{F}_4$ is subsequently processed by a lightweight residual bottleneck.

A $1\times1$ convolution first reduces the number of channels from 768 to 192:

$$
X_r=
\mathrm{GELU}
\left(
\mathrm{LN}
\left(
\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times1}(\widetilde{F}_4)
\right)
\right).
$$

A $3\times3$ depthwise-separable convolution performs spatial transformation in the reduced feature space. The representation is subsequently projected back to 768 channels.

The bottleneck output is formulated as

$$
B=
\widetilde{F}_4+
\gamma_b\mathcal{H}(\widetilde{F}_4),
$$

where $\mathcal{H}(\cdot)$ denotes the bottleneck transformation and $\gamma_b$ is a learnable scalar initialized to zero.

This initialization makes the bottleneck behave initially as an identity mapping and allows its transformation to be progressively introduced during optimization.

\subsection{Residual Frequency-Guided Multi-Scale Convolution}

The principal architectural contribution of this work is the Residual Frequency-Guided Multi-Scale Convolution mechanism.

After the bottleneck feature is upsampled from $H/32$ to $H/16$, the decoder representation is reduced from 768 to 384 channels and concatenated with the Stage-3 encoder feature $F_3$. A lightweight fusion block transforms the resulting 768-channel representation into

$$
X_3\in\mathbb{R}^{384\times H/16\times W/16}.
$$

For a $352\times352$ input,

$$
X_3\in\mathbb{R}^{384\times22\times22}.
$$

A conventional lightweight multi-scale convolution block first expands the feature dimension from $C$ to $2C$:

$$
X_e=
\mathrm{ReLU6}
\left(
\mathrm{BN}
\left(
\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times1}(X_3)
\right)
\right).
$$

Three parallel depthwise convolution branches with kernel sizes

$$
k\in\{1,3,5\}
$$

then generate multi-scale responses,

$$
B_k=\phi_k(X_e),
$$

where each $\phi_k$ consists of a depthwise convolution, Batch Normalization, and ReLU6 activation.

In a conventional MSCB, these branches are combined uniformly:

$$
M_{\mathrm{ord}}
=
B_1+B_3+B_5.
$$

Instead of treating the three receptive fields identically for every image, the proposed mechanism conditions their relative contributions on the bottleneck frequency descriptor $\mathbf{z}_f$.

A lightweight guidance network maps the 1536-dimensional descriptor to three branch logits:

$$
\mathbf{q}
=
W_{g2}
\left(
\mathrm{GELU}
\left(
W_{g1}\mathbf{z}_f
\right)
\right),
$$

where

$$
W_{g1}:1536\rightarrow48,
\qquad
W_{g2}:48\rightarrow3.
$$

The normalized branch weights are calculated as

$$
[\alpha_1,\alpha_3,\alpha_5]
=
3\,\mathrm{Softmax}(\mathbf{q}),
$$

such that

$$
\alpha_1+\alpha_3+\alpha_5=3.
$$

The frequency-guided response becomes

$$
M_{\mathrm{guide}}
=
\alpha_1B_1+
\alpha_3B_3+
\alpha_5B_5.
$$

Directly replacing the conventional multi-scale response with the guided response may excessively perturb an already effective MSCB representation. We therefore introduce the guidance as a bounded residual deviation:

$$
M_{\mathrm{RFG}}
=
M_{\mathrm{ord}}
+
s
\left(
M_{\mathrm{guide}}-M_{\mathrm{ord}}
\right),
$$

where

$$
0\leq s\leq0.5.
$$

The learnable guidance-strength parameter $s$ is initialized to 0.05. Consequently, the frequency-conditioned branch regulation is introduced conservatively during early optimization.

Furthermore, the final guidance-layer weights and biases are initialized such that the initial branch logits are equal. Therefore,

$$
\alpha_1=\alpha_3=\alpha_5=1
$$

at initialization, which gives

$$
M_{\mathrm{guide}}=M_{\mathrm{ord}}.
$$

The module thus initially reproduces the conventional multi-scale path, while optimization is allowed to learn image-dependent deviations from uniform branch weighting.

The resulting feature is processed using channel shuffle and a $1\times1$ projection from $2C$ back to $C$, followed by Batch Normalization. A final residual connection preserves the input representation:

$$
Y_3
=
X_3+
\mathcal{P}
\left(
\mathrm{Shuffle}(M_{\mathrm{RFG}})
\right).
$$

This formulation establishes an explicit cross-level connection between deep frequency information extracted at the bottleneck and multi-scale spatial refinement performed at $H/16$ resolution.

\subsection{Progressive Decoder and Skip Fusion}

After frequency-guided Stage-3 refinement, the decoder progressively restores spatial resolution using bilinear upsampling and depthwise-separable convolution.

The first refined decoder feature has 384 channels:

$$
D_4=Y_3.
$$

The subsequent decoder stages are

$$
D_3=
\mathcal{S}_2
\left[
\mathcal{D}_3(D_4),F_2
\right],
$$

$$
D_2=
\mathcal{S}_1
\left[
\mathcal{D}_2(D_3),F_1
\right],
$$

followed by

$$
D_1=\mathcal{D}_1(D_2).
$$

Their dimensions for a $352\times352$ input are

$$
D_4:
384\times22\times22,
$$

$$
D_3:
192\times44\times44,
$$

$$
D_2:
96\times88\times88,
$$

and

$$
D_1:
96\times176\times176.
$$

At the $F_2$ and $F_1$ skip levels, the upsampled decoder representation is concatenated with the corresponding encoder feature and processed by a lightweight fusion block containing a $1\times1$ projection followed by a depthwise-separable $3\times3$ convolution.

No additional attention mechanism, frequency module, MSCB, or boundary-specific refinement block is applied at these decoder stages.

The final decoder stage has no direct encoder skip connection because the ConvNeXt stem begins at one-quarter of the input resolution.

\subsection{Prediction Head}

The final decoder feature $D_1$ is processed using a lightweight refinement head. Two depthwise-separable $3\times3$ convolutions transform the feature dimensions as

$$
96\rightarrow96\rightarrow48.
$$

Each transformation is followed by Layer Normalization and GELU activation. A final $1\times1$ convolution produces a single-channel segmentation logit map:

$$
P_0=
\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times1}
\left(
\mathcal{R}(D_1)
\right).
$$

The prediction is then bilinearly resized from $H/2\times W/2$ to the original input resolution:

$$
P=
\mathrm{Up}_{H,W}(P_0).
$$

The network directly returns logits. During inference, the foreground probability map is obtained as

$$
\widehat{Y}=\sigma(P),
$$

where $\sigma(\cdot)$ denotes the sigmoid function.

The selected architecture does not employ auxiliary prediction heads or deep supervision.

\subsection{Model Complexity}

The complete proposed model contains

$$
30{,}057{,}415
$$

trainable parameters, corresponding to approximately

$$
30.06\ \mathrm{M}
$$

parameters.

The additional frequency-guided mechanism is introduced only at the first decoder--encoder fusion stage. The remaining decoder retains lightweight depthwise-separable convolution and conventional skip fusion, limiting the architectural modification to the stage at which high-level semantic information is first combined with the relatively high-resolution $F_3$ representation.
