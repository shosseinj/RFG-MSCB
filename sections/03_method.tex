\section{Method}

\subsection{Overall Architecture}

The network uses a U-shaped encoder--decoder architecture built upon a ConvNeXt-Tiny encoder. Frequency information extracted from deep semantic features regulates multi-scale processing during decoding. Frequency enhancement and multi-scale convolution are linked through a cross-level connection between the bottleneck and the first decoder stage.

Let $I \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times H\times W}$ denote an input colonoscopy image. The ConvNeXt-Tiny encoder extracts four hierarchical feature maps,

\begin{equation}
\{F_1,F_2,F_3,F_4\}=E(I),
\label{eq:encoder_features}
\end{equation}

where $F_1\in\mathbb{R}^{96\times H/4\times W/4}$, $F_2\in\mathbb{R}^{192\times H/8\times W/8}$, $F_3\in\mathbb{R}^{384\times H/16\times W/16}$, and $F_4\in\mathbb{R}^{768\times H/32\times W/32}$. For an input resolution of $352\times352$, their spatial dimensions are $88\times88$, $44\times44$, $22\times22$, and $11\times11$, respectively.

The deepest encoder feature $F_4$ is first processed by a Frequency-Aware Feature Enhancement Module (FAFEM), which produces an enhanced bottleneck representation together with a compact frequency descriptor. The enhanced representation is passed through a lightweight residual bottleneck, whereas the frequency descriptor is propagated to the first decoder stage. After the decoder feature is fused with $F_3$, a Residual Frequency-Guided Multi-Scale Convolution Block (RFG-MSCB) uses the frequency descriptor to adaptively regulate its multi-scale convolution branches.

The overall processing can therefore be summarized as

\begin{align}
(\widetilde{F}_4,\mathbf{z}_f)
&=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FAFEM}}(F_4),
\label{eq:overall_fafem}\\
B&=\mathcal{B}(\widetilde{F}_4),
\label{eq:overall_bottleneck}\\
D_4&=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{RFG}}
\left(
\mathcal{S}_3\left[\mathcal{D}_4(B),F_3\right],
\mathbf{z}_f
\right).
\label{eq:overall_stage3}
\end{align}

followed by

\begin{align}
D_3&=\mathcal{S}_2\left[\mathcal{D}_3(D_4),F_2\right],
\label{eq:decoder_stage2}\\
D_2&=\mathcal{S}_1\left[\mathcal{D}_2(D_3),F_1\right],
\label{eq:decoder_stage1}\\
D_1&=\mathcal{D}_1(D_2).
\label{eq:decoder_final}
\end{align}

Here, $\mathcal{D}_i$ denotes a decoder block, $\mathcal{S}_i$ denotes the corresponding skip-feature fusion operation, $\mathbf{z}_f$ is the frequency descriptor extracted at the bottleneck, and $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{RFG}}$ denotes the residual frequency-guided multi-scale refinement mechanism. Equations~\eqref{eq:overall_stage3}--\eqref{eq:decoder_final} summarize the decoder path.

The final prediction head transforms $D_1$ into a single-channel segmentation logit map, which is bilinearly resized to the original image resolution.

\subsection{ConvNeXt-Tiny Encoder}

The encoder employs ConvNeXt-Tiny with stage depths $[3,3,9,3]$ and channel dimensions $[96,192,384,768]$. Before entering the encoder, each RGB image is normalized using the ImageNet statistics $\boldsymbol{\mu}=(0.485,0.456,0.406)$ and $\boldsymbol{\sigma}=(0.229,0.224,0.225)$.

The first stage uses a $4\times4$ convolution with stride 4 to generate $F_1$. Each subsequent stage begins with spatial downsampling using a $2\times2$ stride-2 convolution.

For an input feature $X$, a ConvNeXt block first performs a $7\times7$ depthwise convolution. The resulting feature is normalized in channels-last format and processed by a pointwise multilayer perceptron with a fourfold channel expansion:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Z&=\mathrm{DWConv}_{7\times7}(X),\\
V&=W_2\left(\mathrm{GELU}\left(W_1(\mathrm{LN}(Z))\right)\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:convnext_transform}
\end{equation}

The block output is obtained through layer-scaled residual learning,

\begin{equation}
Y=X+\mathrm{DropPath}(\gamma\odot V),
\label{eq:convnext_residual}
\end{equation}

where $\gamma$ is a learnable layer-scale parameter initialized to $10^{-6}$.

A stochastic-depth rate of 0.25 is used throughout the encoder. Feature dropout with probability 0.2 is additionally applied after the second, third, and fourth encoder stages.

\subsection{Frequency-Aware Bottleneck Enhancement}

The deepest encoder representation $F_4$ contains strong semantic information but has undergone substantial spatial downsampling. To expose complementary frequency characteristics of this representation, FAFEM is applied exclusively at the bottleneck.

Given $X=F_4$, a local low-frequency approximation is obtained using $3\times3$ average pooling,

\begin{align}
X_L&=\mathrm{AvgPool}_{3\times3}(X),
\label{eq:fafem_low_frequency}\\
X_H&=X-X_L.
\label{eq:fafem_high_frequency}
\end{align}

The low- and high-frequency components in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:fafem_low_frequency} and \eqref{eq:fafem_high_frequency} are independently transformed using depthwise convolutional branches,

\begin{equation}
\widehat{X}_L=\phi_L(X_L),
\qquad
\widehat{X}_H=\phi_H(X_H),
\label{eq:fafem_frequency_transforms}
\end{equation}

where each $\phi(\cdot)$ contains a $3\times3$ depthwise convolution followed by GELU activation.

The two components are concatenated and globally pooled to obtain the frequency descriptor

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}_f
=
\mathrm{GAP}
\left(
[\widehat{X}_L,\widehat{X}_H]
\right)
\in\mathbb{R}^{2C}.
\label{eq:frequency_descriptor}
\end{equation}

Since $C=768$ at the bottleneck, $\mathbf{z}_f\in\mathbb{R}^{1536}$.

A lightweight channel-gating network generates adaptive weights for the low- and high-frequency representations. Their weighted combination is spatially refined and introduced through a residual connection:

\begin{equation}
\widetilde{F}_4
=
F_4+
\gamma_f\,\mathcal{R}_f
\left(
w_L\odot\widehat{X}_L+
w_H\odot\widehat{X}_H
\right),
\label{eq:fafem_residual_output}
\end{equation}

where $\gamma_f$ is a learnable scalar initialized to zero.

\subsection{Lightweight Residual Bottleneck}

The frequency-enhanced representation $\widetilde{F}_4$ is subsequently processed by a lightweight residual bottleneck.

A $1\times1$ convolution first reduces the number of channels from 768 to 192:

\begin{equation}
X_r=\mathrm{GELU}\left(\mathrm{LN}\left(
\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times1}(\widetilde{F}_4)\right)\right).
\label{eq:bottleneck_reduction}
\end{equation}

A $3\times3$ depthwise-separable convolution performs spatial transformation in the reduced feature space. The representation is subsequently projected back to 768 channels.

The bottleneck output is formulated as

\begin{equation}
B=
\widetilde{F}_4+
\gamma_b\mathcal{H}(\widetilde{F}_4),
\label{eq:lightweight_bottleneck}
\end{equation}

where $\mathcal{H}(\cdot)$ denotes the bottleneck transformation and $\gamma_b$ is a learnable scalar initialized to zero.

This initialization makes the bottleneck behave initially as an identity mapping and allows its transformation to be progressively introduced during optimization.

\subsection{Residual Frequency-Guided Multi-Scale Convolution}

The principal architectural contribution of this work is the Residual Frequency-Guided Multi-Scale Convolution mechanism.

After the bottleneck feature is upsampled from $H/32$ to $H/16$, the decoder representation is reduced from 768 to 384 channels and concatenated with the Stage-3 encoder feature $F_3$. A lightweight fusion block transforms the resulting 768-channel representation into $X_3\in\mathbb{R}^{384\times H/16\times W/16}$, or $\mathbb{R}^{384\times22\times22}$ for a $352\times352$ input.

A conventional lightweight multi-scale convolution block first expands the feature dimension from $C$ to $2C$:

\begin{equation}
X_e=
\mathrm{ReLU6}
\left(
\mathrm{BN}
\left(
\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times1}(X_3)
\right)
\right).
\label{eq:mscb_expansion}
\end{equation}

Three parallel depthwise convolution branches with kernel sizes $k\in\{1,3,5\}$ then generate multi-scale responses,

\begin{equation}
B_k=\phi_k(X_e),
\label{eq:mscb_branches}
\end{equation}

where each $\phi_k$ consists of a depthwise convolution, Batch Normalization, and ReLU6 activation.

In a conventional MSCB, these branches are combined uniformly:

\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{ord}}
=
B_1+B_3+B_5.
\label{eq:mscb_ordinary_aggregation}
\end{equation}

The new mechanism conditions the relative contributions of the three receptive fields on the bottleneck frequency descriptor $\mathbf{z}_f$.

A lightweight guidance network maps the 1536-dimensional descriptor to three branch logits:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{q}
=
W_{g2}
\left(
\mathrm{GELU}
\left(
W_{g1}\mathbf{z}_f
\right)
\right),
\label{eq:rfg_logits}
\end{equation}

where $W_{g1}:1536\rightarrow48$ and $W_{g2}:48\rightarrow3$.

The normalized branch weights are calculated as

\begin{equation}
[\alpha_1,\alpha_3,\alpha_5]
=
3\,\mathrm{Softmax}(\mathbf{q}),
\label{eq:rfg_weights}
\end{equation}

such that $\alpha_1+\alpha_3+\alpha_5=3$.

The frequency-guided response becomes

\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{guide}}
=
\alpha_1B_1+
\alpha_3B_3+
\alpha_5B_5.
\label{eq:rfg_guided_aggregation}
\end{equation}

We introduce the guidance as a bounded residual deviation from the conventional multi-scale response:

\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{RFG}}
=
M_{\mathrm{ord}}
+
s
\left(
M_{\mathrm{guide}}-M_{\mathrm{ord}}
\right),
\label{eq:rfg_residual}
\end{equation}

where $0\leq s\leq0.5$.

The learnable guidance-strength parameter $s$ is initialized to 0.05.

The final guidance-layer weights and biases are initialized so that the branch logits are equal. Thus, at initialization, $\alpha_1=\alpha_3=\alpha_5=1$ and $M_{\mathrm{guide}}=M_{\mathrm{ord}}$.

The module thus initially reproduces the conventional multi-scale path, while optimization is allowed to learn image-dependent deviations from uniform branch weighting.

The resulting feature is processed using channel shuffle and a $1\times1$ projection from $2C$ back to $C$, followed by Batch Normalization. A final residual connection preserves the input representation:

\begin{equation}
Y_3
=
X_3+
\mathcal{P}
\left(
\mathrm{Shuffle}(M_{\mathrm{RFG}})
\right).
\label{eq:rfg_final_output}
\end{equation}

This formulation connects the bottleneck frequency descriptor to multi-scale spatial refinement at $H/16$ resolution.

\subsection{Progressive Decoder and Skip Fusion}

After frequency-guided Stage-3 refinement, the decoder progressively restores spatial resolution using bilinear upsampling and depthwise-separable convolution.

The first refined decoder feature has 384 channels, $D_4=Y_3$.

The subsequent decoder stages follow Eqs.~\eqref{eq:decoder_stage2}--\eqref{eq:decoder_final}.

For a $352\times352$ input, the decoder dimensions are $D_4:384\times22\times22$, $D_3:192\times44\times44$, $D_2:96\times88\times88$, and $D_1:96\times176\times176$.

At the $F_2$ and $F_1$ skip levels, the upsampled decoder representation is concatenated with the corresponding encoder feature and processed by a lightweight fusion block containing a $1\times1$ projection followed by a depthwise-separable $3\times3$ convolution.

No additional attention mechanism, frequency module, MSCB, or boundary-specific refinement block is applied at these decoder stages.

The final decoder stage has no direct encoder skip connection because the ConvNeXt stem begins at one-quarter of the input resolution.

\subsection{Prediction Head}

The final decoder feature $D_1$ is processed using a lightweight refinement head. Two depthwise-separable $3\times3$ convolutions transform the feature dimensions as $96\rightarrow96\rightarrow48$.

Each transformation is followed by Layer Normalization and GELU activation. A final $1\times1$ convolution produces a single-channel segmentation logit map:

\begin{align}
P_0&=\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times1}\left(\mathcal{R}(D_1)\right),
\label{eq:prediction_logits}\\
P&=\mathrm{Up}_{H,W}(P_0),
\label{eq:prediction_resize}\\
\widehat{Y}&=\sigma(P).
\label{eq:prediction_probability}
\end{align}

The prediction in Eq.~\eqref{eq:prediction_resize} is bilinearly resized from $H/2\times W/2$ to the original input resolution. The network directly returns logits; during inference, Eq.~\eqref{eq:prediction_probability} gives the foreground probability map,

where $\sigma(\cdot)$ denotes the sigmoid function.

The selected architecture does not employ auxiliary prediction heads or deep supervision.

with THOP on a $1\times3\times352\times352$ input gives 13.91
GMACs, corresponding to 27.82 GFLOPs under the project convention
$\mathrm{FLOPs}=2\times\mathrm{MACs}$. Table~\ref{tab:model-complexity}

The remaining decoder retains depthwise-separable convolution and conventional skip fusion, limiting the architectural modification to the stage at which high-level semantic information is first combined with the relatively high-resolution $F_3$ representation.
