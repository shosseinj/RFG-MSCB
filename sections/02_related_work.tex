\section{Related work}\label{related-work}

\subsection{Convolutional encoder-decoder
architectures}\label{convolutional-encoder-decoder-architectures}

Fully convolutional encoder-decoder networks form the foundation of
modern medical image segmentation. U-Net introduced a symmetric
contracting and expanding architecture in which high-resolution encoder
representations are transferred to the decoder through skip connections.
This design enables the network to combine contextual representations
with spatially detailed features and has become a standard baseline for
biomedical segmentation {[}1{]}.

Subsequent studies modified the U-shaped architecture to improve feature
fusion and optimization. UNet++ introduced nested dense skip pathways to
reduce the semantic discrepancy between encoder and decoder features and
employed deep supervision at multiple decoder depths {[}2{]}. Such
developments demonstrated that the design of skip connections is as
important as the choice of encoder because direct feature transfer may
combine representations with substantially different semantic
characteristics.

In polyp segmentation, computationally efficient convolutional networks
have also received considerable attention. HarDNet-MSEG combines a
low-memory-traffic convolutional backbone with a lightweight decoder and
illustrates that competitive segmentation performance can be obtained
without a computationally expensive transformer architecture {[}6{]}.
However, standard convolutional models generally require additional
mechanisms to capture broad contextual relationships and refine weak
lesion boundaries.

PraNet introduced a parallel partial decoder to aggregate high-level
features and a reverse-attention strategy to progressively refine
uncertain polyp regions {[}7{]}. Its recurrent interaction between
regional predictions and boundary cues demonstrated the effectiveness of
explicitly directing the decoder toward inadequately segmented regions.
Nevertheless, reverse-attention refinement depends on preceding
prediction maps and does not directly solve the problem of noisy
information transferred through conventional skip connections.

\subsection{Global and multi-scale contextual
modeling}\label{global-and-multi-scale-contextual-modeling}

The considerable variation in polyp size motivates the use of
multi-scale contextual modeling. A segmentation network must preserve
local details for small lesions while maintaining a sufficiently large
receptive field to identify extensive or irregularly shaped polyps.
Dilated convolutions, pyramid pooling, multi-branch receptive fields,
and cross-scale feature aggregation have consequently been incorporated
into many segmentation architectures.

Transformer-based approaches address this problem by modeling long-range
spatial relationships. TransUNet uses a transformer encoder over
convolutional feature representations to capture global context while
retaining high-resolution CNN features for localization {[}3{]}.
Polyp-PVT employs a pyramid vision transformer and specialized fusion
modules to integrate semantic and positional information across
hierarchical feature levels {[}4{]}. These methods improve long-range
feature interaction, but their attention operations and cross-level
fusion mechanisms may increase architectural and computational
complexity.

Recent contextual-information-flow and multi-stage architectures
similarly emphasize the need to coordinate local lesion appearance with
broader anatomical context. State-space and frequency-oriented
architectures have also been investigated. Polyp-Mamba combines
multi-frequency perception with a gated selection mechanism to model
spatial dependencies and frequency-sensitive representations {[}8{]}.
The present work does not employ frequency decomposition or a
state-space operator; instead, it uses parallel dilated depthwise
convolutions and global average pooling to obtain multi-scale contextual
information within a purely convolutional framework.

\subsection{Boundary-aware polyp
segmentation}\label{boundary-aware-polyp-segmentation}

Weak and visually ambiguous lesion boundaries are among the principal
difficulties in colorectal polyp segmentation. Boundary-aware networks
attempt to preserve or reconstruct structural information that may
disappear during repeated down-sampling. These methods commonly
introduce explicit edge supervision, auxiliary contour branches,
gradient-derived features, reverse attention, or boundary-sensitive
feature fusion.

MEGANet employs multi-scale edge-guided attention and incorporates
high-frequency information derived from image pyramids to improve
segmentation of weak boundaries {[}9{]}. Its findings demonstrate that
explicitly retaining edge-related information can reduce deterioration
of boundary features in deep networks. MSBP-Net performs multi-scale
boundary prediction to improve contour delineation while maintaining
relatively low model complexity {[}10{]}. This line of research
indicates that boundary information should be modeled at more than one
scale because polyp contours may contain both broad structural
transitions and small irregularities.

Explicit edge extraction may also respond to irrelevant structures such
as folds, reflections, bubbles, and instruments. Furthermore, additional
boundary labels or edge-generation procedures may increase the
complexity of the training pipeline. The BSEI module in the proposed
model follows a different strategy. It derives a learned spatial
response from transformed skip features and uses this response to
modulate the features through residual enhancement. Therefore, the
method is boundary-sensitive but does not depend on Sobel filtering,
Laplacian pyramids, explicit contour labels, or a separate boundary
loss.

\subsection{Detail preservation and adaptive feature
fusion}\label{detail-preservation-and-adaptive-feature-fusion}

Deep semantic features provide strong category discrimination but have
reduced spatial resolution. Conversely, shallow features preserve local
appearance and edge information but may contain substantial background
noise. Effective segmentation therefore requires controlled interaction
between semantic and detailed representations.

Conventional U-shaped networks concatenate or add shallow and deep
features directly. Although these operations are computationally simple,
they assign no explicit relevance to detailed information. Attention and
gating mechanisms provide a more selective alternative by estimating the
contribution of one representation conditioned on another. Recent
polyp-segmentation architectures employ specialized cross-scale or
attention-based fusion to suppress irrelevant responses and improve
lesion localization.

The proposed method addresses detail preservation using a shallow Detail
Branch operating at one-half of the input resolution. Instead of
directly adding this feature to the decoder, the Gated Detail Fusion
module estimates a gate from the concatenated decoder and detail
representations. The gate modulates the detail feature before final
fusion, enabling the network to retain high-resolution information while
limiting transfer of irrelevant low-level responses.

\subsection{Positioning of the proposed
method}\label{positioning-of-the-proposed-method}

Existing approaches have improved polyp segmentation through reverse
attention, transformer-based contextual modeling, frequency processing,
explicit boundary prediction, or complex multi-stage refinement.
Nevertheless, many methods address context, boundaries, and fine details
as separate problems. The proposed architecture integrates these
requirements within a unified convolutional encoder-decoder framework.

Unlike transformer-based methods, the model employs a ConvNeXt-Tiny
encoder and depthwise separable convolutions. Unlike frequency-oriented
networks, it does not require Fourier or wavelet decomposition. Unlike
explicitly supervised boundary networks, it derives a learned
local-response map from intermediate features. Finally, unlike direct
shallow-deep fusion, it uses a conditioned gate to control the
contribution of the high-resolution detail representation. These design
choices provide a coherent framework for investigating whether
multi-scale context, boundary-sensitive skip refinement, and selective
detail fusion can jointly improve polyp segmentation and cross-dataset
generalization.
