\section{Related work}\label{related-work}

\subsection{Encoder--decoder architectures for polyp segmentation}

Fully convolutional encoder--decoder networks remain a central design pattern for
biomedical segmentation. U-Net combines semantic context with spatial detail through
skip connections, while UNet++ uses nested dense pathways to reduce the semantic gap
between encoder and decoder representations \cite{unet,unetpp}. Polyp-specific
convolutional networks subsequently developed this pattern in different directions:
HarDNet-MSEG couples a low-memory-traffic backbone with lightweight decoding, whereas
PraNet combines a parallel partial decoder with reverse attention for progressive
region refinement \cite{hardnetmseg,pranet}. More recent designs emphasize
cross-attention and feature exploration, multi-level lesion awareness, progressive
coarse-to-fine decoding, or dual-view feature propagation
\cite{cafenet,mlbnet,mspn,dvfipnet}. These studies demonstrate that cross-level reuse
and staged refinement are important for heterogeneous polyp appearance. They do not,
however, establish the specific bottleneck-to-decoder interaction considered here, in
which a deep frequency descriptor controls the receptive-field mixture at an earlier
decoder fusion.

Modern convolutional encoders offer a complementary way to strengthen this design
without replacing the full hierarchy by self-attention. ConvNeXt modernizes a ConvNet
using large-kernel depthwise convolution, inverted channel expansion, Layer
Normalization, GELU, layer scaling, and stochastic depth \cite{convnext}. Its
hierarchical outputs are suitable for U-shaped feature reuse, while depthwise spatial
operators also support economical dense prediction. The present framework therefore
uses ConvNeXt-Tiny as an established encoder and localizes its contribution to the
coupling between bottleneck frequency processing and Stage-3 decoder refinement.

\subsection{Transformer and hybrid contextual segmentation}

Transformer and hybrid models address the broad variation in polyp size, morphology,
and contrast by combining local appearance with long-range context. TransUNet provides
an influential CNN--transformer formulation for medical segmentation, and Polyp-PVT
adapts hierarchical pyramid-transformer features to polyp segmentation
\cite{transunet,polyppvt}. Recent polyp methods extend this direction through
contrastive transformer representations in CTNet, lightweight vision transformers in
Polyp-LVT, contextual information flow in CIFFormer, and phase-wise feature-pyramid
retention in PFPRNet \cite{ctnet,polyplvt,cifformer,pfprnet}. Multi-feature pyramid
fusion has likewise been combined with visual transformers in MF-Net
\cite{mfnet}. Collectively, these methods prioritize global dependency modeling and
cross-scale semantic exchange, but often employ attention or transformer machinery
throughout substantial parts of the network. Our method instead retains a
convolutional ConvNeXt encoder--decoder and introduces one lightweight, descriptor-
conditioned multi-scale refinement at Stage 3.

\subsection{Efficient multi-scale convolutional decoding}

Multi-scale decoding is particularly relevant to polyps, which may be small and
low-contrast or occupy much of the endoscopic frame. Parallel kernels provide
different effective receptive fields, while depthwise convolution limits the cost of
spatial filtering. EMCAD formalized this principle in an efficient medical-image
decoder whose Multi-Scale Convolution Blocks (MSCBs) expand the feature, process it
with multiple depthwise kernels, aggregate the branch responses, shuffle channels,
and project the result \cite{emcad}. We adopt the lightweight MSCB topology as the
ordinary multi-scale reference path rather than presenting it as a new block.

The broader adaptive-convolution literature shows that receptive fields need not be
combined uniformly. Selective Kernel Networks use a feature descriptor to select
among kernel scales, and Dynamic Convolution forms input-dependent mixtures over
convolution kernels \cite{sknet,dynamicconv}. These mechanisms provide direct
precedent for adaptive branch weighting, but their routing signals are formed from
the features being transformed at the same level. RFG-MSCB instead transfers a
low/high-frequency descriptor from the bottleneck to the Stage-3 decoder fusion. It
uses this cross-level signal to weight the adopted MSCB's $1\times1$, $3\times3$, and
$5\times5$ depthwise branches, while retaining their ordinary sum as an explicit
reference response.

\subsection{Frequency-aware and adaptive feature recalibration}

Compact descriptors are widely used to recalibrate convolutional representations.
Squeeze-and-Excitation Networks learn channel-wise gates from global pooled features,
whereas FcaNet interprets channel attention in terms of selected frequency components
\cite{senet,fcanet}. Frequency-aware processing has also moved beyond channel
attention: Frequency-Aware Feature Fusion addresses the loss of high-frequency detail
and inconsistency introduced when fusing features of different resolutions in dense
prediction \cite{freqfusion}. In polyp segmentation, Polyp-Mamba combines
multi-frequency perception with gated selection, providing a recent domain-specific
example of frequency-sensitive contextual modeling \cite{polypmamba}.

These precedents differ from the role assigned to frequency information in the
present framework. The adopted bottleneck FAFEM produces local low-pass and residual
high-pass responses and a pooled descriptor $\mathbf{z}_f$. Rather than using that
descriptor only for channel or spatial recalibration at its source level, RFG-MSCB
propagates it across levels to control the Stage-3 multi-kernel mixture. Moreover, the
guided mixture does not replace ordinary MSCB aggregation: it is introduced as a
bounded residual deviation around that path. Thus, the claimed contribution is the
cross-level residual frequency-guided coupling, not frequency decomposition,
descriptor-based attention, or MSCB individually.

\subsection{Boundary-aware and recent polyp-specific refinement}

Weak boundaries and ambiguous foreground appearance have motivated complementary
polyp-specific refinement strategies. MEGANet applies multi-scale edge-guided
attention, while MSBP-Net predicts boundaries at multiple scales
\cite{meganet,msbpnet}. MLB-Net combines lesion-aware and boundary-enhanced
processing, and PraNet-V2 extends reverse attention with dual supervision
\cite{mlbnet,pranetv2}. Reliability has also received increasing attention: MEIN
models multi-scale uncertainty through evidence integration rather than treating all
predictions as equally certain \cite{mein}. Together with CIFFormer, PFPRNet, MSPN,
and DVFIP-Net, these 2025--2026 methods illustrate a shift toward explicit contextual
flow, hierarchical refinement, uncertainty modeling, and targeted boundary recovery
\cite{cifformer,pfprnet,mspn,dvfipnet}.

Our architecture does not add an edge branch, contour decoder, or uncertainty head;
boundary emphasis is supplied by the training objective. Architecturally, it addresses
a different question: how bottleneck frequency composition can condition receptive-
field selection at a specific decoder level without discarding a standard efficient
multi-scale path. Accordingly, we adopt ConvNeXt-Tiny, a locally implemented FAFEM,
and the MSCB structure from EMCAD, and propose only their cross-level, bounded
residual frequency-guided coupling developed in the next section.
