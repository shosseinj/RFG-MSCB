\section{Ablation Study}\label{ablation-study}

The ablation design compares four configurations in sequence: Baseline,
FAFEM, FAFEM + MSCB, and Residual RFG-MSCB (Ours). This sequence first isolates
bottleneck frequency-aware enhancement, then adds Stage-3 multi-scale refinement,
and finally assesses the residual frequency-guided modification.

\subsection{Ablation Results}

The Baseline serves as the reference configuration without FAFEM and MSCB. Adding
FAFEM introduces bottleneck frequency-aware enhancement, while FAFEM + MSCB further
adds Stage-3 multi-scale convolutional refinement. Residual RFG-MSCB then introduces
cross-level frequency-guided coupling, allowing the bottleneck descriptor to modulate
the multi-scale branches at the Stage-3 decoder fusion. Table~\ref{tab:ablation}
reports mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation across three seed-level macro
averages. Each seed-level value is the unweighted arithmetic mean across
Kvasir-SEG, CVC-ClinicDB, CVC-300, CVC-ColonDB, and ETIS-LaribPolypDB.

\subsection{Ablation Interpretation}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} presents the macro-averaged ablation results across the five polyp segmentation datasets. Relative to the Baseline, FAFEM increases mDice and mIoU by 3.7 and 3.5 percentage points, respectively. The corresponding changes are +3.0 points in $F_{\beta}^{w}$, +1.8 in $S_{\alpha}$, +2.7 in $mE_{\phi}$, and +2.9 in $\max E_{\phi}$, while MAE decreases by 0.1 point from 0.019 to 0.018.

Adding MSCB to FAFEM raises mDice, mIoU, and $F_{\beta}^{w}$ by 0.3, 0.5, and 0.4 percentage points, respectively. The increases in $S_{\alpha}$, $mE_{\phi}$, and $\max E_{\phi}$ are 0.2, 0.3, and 0.1 points, while the displayed MAE remains 0.018.

Relative to FAFEM + MSCB, Residual RFG-MSCB adds 0.5 points in mDice, 0.8 in mIoU, 0.4 in $F_{\beta}^{w}$, 0.4 in $S_{\alpha}$, 0.4 in $mE_{\phi}$, and 0.3 in $\max E_{\phi}$. It consequently has the highest mean for each of these six metrics: 0.872, 0.810, 0.849, 0.914, 0.941, and 0.949, respectively. Its displayed MAE remains 0.018, tied with FAFEM and FAFEM + MSCB.

The progression increases parameters from 29.190 M for the Baseline to 29.360 M with FAFEM, 29.983 M with MSCB, and 30.057 M with Residual RFG-MSCB. GFLOPs increase from 27.202 to 27.208 and 27.818 after adding MSCB, then remain 27.818 for the final configuration at the displayed precision. Thus the complete configuration adds 0.867 M parameters and 0.616 GFLOPs over the Baseline. Relative to FAFEM + MSCB, the residual frequency-guided extension adds about 0.074 M parameters while leaving GFLOPs unchanged at the displayed precision. Under matched FP16 inference, latency increases from 11.630 to 11.806 ms (1.52\%), FPS decreases from 85.986 to 84.701 (1.50\%), and peak allocated VRAM increases from 252.876 to 253.306 MiB (0.17\%). This represents modest measured runtime overhead, with throughput remaining 84.70 FPS. Together with the metric changes, this four-configuration progression supports positive descriptive contributions from each added component at the reported aggregate level. No statistical significance is claimed.

\subsection{MSCB-lite Placement Analysis}
\label{mscb-placement}

Since Table~\ref{tab:ablation} identifies multi-scale refinement as an effective
component, we further examined whether extending MSCB-lite beyond the Stage-3 fusion
provides additional benefit. The matched comparison evaluates refinement at Stage 3,
Stages 3 + 2, and Stages 3 + 2 + 1 using the same five-dataset macro-averaging
procedure. The overlap metrics are summarized across the evaluated seeds.

Stage 3 alone has the highest mean mDice (0.8685) and mean mIoU (0.8027), as well as
the lowest parameter count (29.983 M) and GFLOPs (27.818). Extending MSCB-lite to
additional stages does not improve the mean overlap metrics, while parameters increase
to 30.148 M and 30.193 M and GFLOPs increase to 28.468 and 29.197. Stage 3 therefore
provides the most favorable observed accuracy--complexity trade-off among the tested
MSCB-lite placements. These descriptive comparisons do not establish statistical
significance. This placement behavior provides empirical motivation
for retaining multi-scale refinement at Stage 3 when introducing the residual
frequency-guided formulation, while avoiding the additional cost of distributing MSCB
across shallower decoder levels. Residual RFG-MSCB itself was not exhaustively tested
at alternative decoder-stage placements.

\subsection{Qualitative Ablation Analysis}

Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative_ablation} shows the Baseline, FAFEM, FAFEM + MSCB,
and Residual RFG-MSCB predictions in the same order as the quantitative ablation.
The final column provides an additional spatial visualization associated with the final
prediction. Because the original heatmap-generation metadata are not available in the
current experiment artifacts, we use the visualization only as a qualitative spatial
reference and do not assign it a specific interpretability mechanism.
The added components alter mask coverage and boundary position across the displayed
examples. In the CVC-ColonDB case with an elongated annotation, the disconnected
Baseline regions are replaced by more coherent predictions from FAFEM, FAFEM + MSCB,
and Residual RFG-MSCB.

Residual RFG-MSCB yields a more coherent or better-localized mask in selected cases,
including the displayed CVC-ColonDB case, while its effect varies among the selected
images. In the displayed Kvasir-SEG, CVC-ClinicDB, CVC-300, and small-lesion ETIS
examples, the four configurations differ mainly in boundary position and extent, and
no configuration reproduces every ground-truth contour exactly. These examples
therefore provide a visual complement to
the aggregate trends in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, rather than evidence of uniform
improvement for every image.
