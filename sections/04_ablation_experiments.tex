\section{Ablation Study}\label{ablation-study}

The ablation design compares four configurations in sequence: Baseline,
FAFEM, FAFEM + MSCB, and Residual RFG-MSCB (Ours). This sequence first isolates
bottleneck frequency-aware enhancement, then adds Stage-3 multi-scale refinement,
and finally assesses the residual frequency-guided modification.

\subsection{Ablation Results}

The Baseline serves as the reference configuration without FAFEM and MSCB. Adding
FAFEM introduces bottleneck frequency-aware enhancement, while FAFEM + MSCB further
adds Stage-3 multi-scale convolutional refinement. Residual RFG-MSCB then introduces
cross-level frequency-guided coupling, allowing the bottleneck descriptor to modulate
the multi-scale branches at the Stage-3 decoder fusion. Table~\ref{tab:ablation}
reports mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation across three seed-level macro
averages. Each seed-level value is the unweighted arithmetic mean across
Kvasir-SEG, CVC-ClinicDB, CVC-300, CVC-ColonDB, and ETIS-LaribPolypDB.

\subsection{Ablation Interpretation}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} presents the macro-averaged ablation results across the five polyp segmentation datasets. Relative to the Baseline, FAFEM increases mDice and mIoU by 3.7 and 3.5 percentage points, respectively. The corresponding changes are +3.0 points in $F_{\beta}^{w}$, +1.8 in $S_{\alpha}$, +2.7 in $mE_{\phi}$, and +2.9 in $\max E_{\phi}$, while MAE decreases by 0.1 point from 0.019 to 0.018.

Adding MSCB to FAFEM raises mDice, mIoU, and $F_{\beta}^{w}$ by 0.3, 0.5, and 0.4 percentage points, respectively. The increases in $S_{\alpha}$, $mE_{\phi}$, and $\max E_{\phi}$ are 0.2, 0.3, and 0.1 points, while the displayed MAE remains 0.018.

Relative to FAFEM + MSCB, Residual RFG-MSCB adds 0.5 points in mDice, 0.8 in mIoU, 0.4 in $F_{\beta}^{w}$, 0.4 in $S_{\alpha}$, 0.4 in $mE_{\phi}$, and 0.3 in $\max E_{\phi}$. It consequently has the highest mean for each of these six metrics: 0.872, 0.810, 0.849, 0.914, 0.941, and 0.949, respectively. Its displayed MAE remains 0.018, tied with FAFEM and FAFEM + MSCB.

The progression increases parameters from 29.19 M for the Baseline to 29.36 M with FAFEM, 29.98 M with MSCB, and 30.06 M with Residual RFG-MSCB. GFLOPs increase from 27.202 to 27.208, 27.514, and 27.818, respectively. Thus the complete configuration adds 0.87 M parameters and 0.616 GFLOPs over the Baseline; the final residual frequency-guided step accounts for 0.08 M parameters and 0.304 GFLOPs. Together with the metric changes, this four-stage pattern supports positive descriptive contributions from each added component at the reported aggregate level. No statistical significance is claimed.

\subsection{MSCB-lite Placement Analysis}
\label{mscb-placement}

Since Table~\ref{tab:ablation} identifies multi-scale refinement as an effective
component, we further examined whether extending MSCB-lite beyond the Stage-3 fusion
provides additional benefit. The matched comparison evaluates refinement at Stage 3,
Stages 3 + 2, and Stages 3 + 2 + 1 using the same five-dataset macro-averaging
procedure.

Stage 3 alone gives the highest observed mDice (0.8685) and mIoU (0.8027).
Extending MSCB-lite to Stage 2 decreases both overlap metrics; extension to Stage 1
recovers part of that loss but does not exceed Stage 3 alone. Meanwhile, GFLOPs rise
from 27.818 to 28.468 and 29.197, with corresponding parameter increases. Stage 3
therefore provides the most favorable observed accuracy--complexity trade-off among
the tested MSCB-lite placements. Based on this placement behavior, multi-scale
refinement was retained only at Stage 3 when introducing the residual frequency-guided
formulation, avoiding the additional cost of distributing MSCB across shallower decoder
levels.

\subsection{Qualitative Ablation Analysis}

Relative to the Baseline, FAFEM + MSCB produces mask shapes with closer spatial
coverage or boundary alignment in several displayed examples. In the displayed
CVC-ColonDB case with an elongated annotation, the disconnected Baseline regions are
replaced by a more coherent prediction. For the displayed small-lesion
ETIS-LaribPolypDB example, the Baseline does not recover the annotated region,
whereas both augmented configurations produce a localized response.

Residual RFG-MSCB yields a more coherent or better-localized mask in selected cases,
including the displayed CVC-ColonDB case, while its effect varies among the selected
images. In the displayed Kvasir-SEG, CVC-ClinicDB, and CVC-300 examples, the predictions
differ mainly in boundary position and extent, and no configuration reproduces every
ground-truth contour exactly. These examples therefore provide a visual complement to
the aggregate trends in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, rather than evidence of uniform
improvement for every image.
